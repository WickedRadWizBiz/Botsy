# Market research elsewhere: off-Amazon book-demand signals

> **Status: design, 2026-10-17, skeptic-checked, not built.** Answers the user's "Apify can be used to do market research elsewhere". Every site below was checked against CLAUDE.md's platform rule (terms + robots.txt, recorded in `config/platform_policies.yaml`); the terms quoted were re-read on 2026-10-17 unless a line says otherwise. "Estimate" and "unverified" mean exactly that.

## 1. Honest first

For KDP low-content books (planners, log books, journals, coloring and activity books) **Amazon is the market**. No other site tells you what sells there. Other sites can tell you two useful but smaller things: **topic interest** (are people searching for or talking about "fishing log"?) and **catalog saturation** (how many such books already exist, how fast new ones appear, how long they are). Those numbers can rank which Amazon searches to look at first and suggest adjacent ideas; they can never say a book will sell, and they never make a brief by themselves.

A second honest point: most consumer sites forbid automated access in their terms, and "it is just Apify" does not change that. Of everything reviewed, only two sources are both official and free for this use: **Open Library's Search API** and the **Google Books API**. So Apify has nothing to do here yet.

## 3. What to build

**Build (small, about 1 day):** `research/signals/` with two tiny clients and one aggregator.
- `openlibrary.py`: `GET https://openlibrary.org/search.json?q=<term>&fields=publisher,first_publish_year,number_of_pages_median,edition_count&limit=100`, header `User-Agent: Botsy/<version> (<BOTSY_CONTACT_EMAIL from .env>)`, at most one call a second, one page per term per week. Before the first scheduled run the user sends a short note to openlibrary@archive.org describing the volume (their page asks regular users to identify themselves and names that address).
- `googlebooks.py`: `GET https://www.googleapis.com/books/v1/volumes?q=<term>&printType=books&orderBy=newest&maxResults=40&key=<GOOGLE_BOOKS_API_KEY>`, two pages per term per week (about 30 to 40 calls a week), with `fields=` to keep responses small. Titles are read in memory and discarded; only counts are stored.
- Aggregator output per KDP search term, per week, into `data/history/<date>/kdp_signals.json` (gitignored like the rest of history): `catalog_total`, `new_12m`, `new_24m`, `kdp_share` (publisher is "Independently published"), `pages_median`, `pages_p25_p75`, `years_histogram`, `source_counts`. **No titles, authors, ISBNs or links.** Because Open Library runs about two years behind, `new_12m` comes mainly from Google Books; the report says which source each number came from.

**How the signals enter Botsy without weakening the 10/3 floor:**
1. **Ranking the KDP search list.** Terms with many recent KDP titles are crowded; terms with steady older titles and few new ones are candidates; terms with almost nothing are either empty markets or not books at all. The weekly list orders the 15 searches by this, and the TinyFish reader (docs/KDP_TINYFISH.md) and your BookBeam exports follow that order. The signal changes which terms get looked at, not what counts as evidence.
2. **Candidates for the Scout / Hermes "adjacent ideas"** (docs/SNN.md): a phrase that is a near neighbour of our own winner and shows catalog movement becomes a candidate for the **existing** exploratory lines (at most 3 in the weekly list: 2 from the SNN, 1 from the counter). The signals rank candidates within those slots; they never add slots. Hermes may point to these lines; it cannot plan a product from them.
3. **A small `catalog_boost` (0.1) on the KDP opportunity score**, exactly like `trend_boost` today: it can close at most 10% of a theme's gap to full demand and is applied only to themes that already passed the 10-book / 3-author floor from Amazon-side rows. It never creates support rows, never counts as a book or an author, and is reported in the brief as "catalog signal: +0.0x".
4. **Never:** a brief from signals alone; signal titles into any prompt; signals replacing the Amazon rows.

**Do not build:** anything on Reddit, Pinterest, Barnes & Noble, eBay, Lulu, Blurb, WorldCat, Bookshop.org or the Library of Congress; no Apify actors on any of them; no "topic" scraping of blogs or forums. Pinterest Trends and Reddit stay things you can read by hand; if you want Pinterest's seasonal curves in Botsy, a typed template is the compliant way.

**Settings:** `signals: {open_library: {enabled: true, per_second: 1}, google_books: {enabled: true, pages_per_term: 2}, catalog_boost: 0.1, exploratory_within_snn_slots: true}`; keys `GOOGLE_BOOKS_API_KEY` and `BOTSY_CONTACT_EMAIL` in `.env` (and `.env.example`). The signals job runs inside the weekly search-list step (before the list is written), so the week's list is already ranked.
